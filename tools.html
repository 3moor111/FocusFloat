Focus Timer

A timer that floats over your work.

Start a session and a small countdown pill floats above every app: your PDF reader, a recorded lecture, the browser. It keeps counting with the screen off and comes back after the phone restarts.

  • Tap the time to slide out pause, reset, open app, hide at edge and close.
  • Swipe the pill sideways, or drag it to an edge, and it tucks into the edge as a thin flap with the digits blurred. Tap the flap to bring it back.
  • Drag the pill and a bin appears at the bottom; drop it there to close the timer.
  • When time is up the pill pulses red with an alarm, and offers +15m, +30m or +1h of extra time.
  • Dark or light pill style under Focus Timer › Timer & flap style.
  • Optional: block apps for the same time, with the switch on the timer screen.
How to use it: Home › Focus (the big button in the bottom bar), pick a length (or 30m / 1h / 2h), then Start floating timer.
Focus Timer

Focus until a clock time.

Instead of a length, choose when to stop, for example “until 6:00 PM”. FocusFloat shows how long that is from now and sets the exact length when you tap Start.

  • Shows “Today · 1h 42m from now”. A time that has already passed today means tomorrow.
  • Follows your phone’s 12- or 24-hour setting.
  • Blocking, breaks and extra time work the same way.
How to use it: Focus Timer › Until a time, tap the big time to pick it, then Start.
App Blocker

Distracting apps stay closed.

Choose the apps you lose time in. During a session they can’t open, and their notifications are held back until you’re done.

  • Checks the app on screen twice a second. A blocked app is sent away and a calm quote screen appears, usually within half a second.
  • The quote screen shows the time left, one of 120 motivational lines, Back to focus, and a break button.
  • Notifications from blocked apps are snoozed, not deleted, so they come back after the session.
  • The phone app, the home screen, Settings and FocusFloat itself can never be blocked, so emergency calls always work.
  • While a session runs, the block list is locked.
How to use it: More › App Blocker, pick apps by category (Social, Messaging, Video, Games, Shopping, Browsers, Music, News…), choose a length from 30 minutes to 4 hours, choose an exit level, then Start blocking.

Ongoing notifications (music, calls, downloads) can’t be hidden. Some phone brands close background apps; allow Autostart and unrestricted battery in Setup.

App Blocker

Decide how hard quitting is, before you start.

Before a blocking session starts you choose an exit level. It’s chosen now, so future-you can’t change it mid-session.

  • Gentle: confirm once to end the session.
  • Pause & reflect: wait 30 seconds before you can end it. The wait restarts if you leave.
  • Prove it: type “I choose to stop focusing right now.” exactly and solve 3 sums.
  • Locked: no way out and no pausing until time is up. Calls always work.
  • Reset, close and dragging to the bin all go through the exit level during a blocking session.
How to use it: The level sheet appears when you start blocking, or when you start a timer with blocking switched on.

Android always lets a person force-stop or uninstall an app in Settings. FocusFloat makes quitting take real effort; it can’t make it impossible.

ScrollStop

Keep the chat. Lose the feed.

ScrollStop stops endless short-video feeds while the rest of the app keeps working. Messages, posts and search are left alone.

  • Works in Instagram (Reels tab covered, Reels viewer stopped), TikTok (For You feed stopped, messages still work), YouTube Shorts, Facebook Reels and Snapchat Spotlight, each with its own switch.
  • When: Off, Always (all day or between set hours), or With sessions (on while a focus session or schedule runs, paused on breaks).
  • When it stops a feed it shows its own page: a quote, today’s count, Back to the app (its chats or home, not the feed), Start a focus session instead, or Close the app.
  • One Reel Pass: a reel someone sends you in a chat plays once. Swiping to the next one sends you back.
  • Weakening it while it’s on all the time asks for a level: Gentle, Wait 30 s, Prove it (type a sentence) or Locked until its hours end.
  • Reads English and Arabic labels.
How to use it: More › ScrollStop. Turn it on in Accessibility once (the screen guides you), choose when it runs and which apps it covers.

These apps change their screens from time to time, so ScrollStop may miss a tab after an update. Use More › ScrollStop › Scan this screen to record the layout (names and positions only, no pictures) and send it so the rules can be fixed.

Schedules

Blocking that starts on its own.

Plan focus time ahead. A schedule starts by itself at its time, even with the app closed, and blocks the apps in your block list.

  • Repeat on chosen weekdays (e.g. Sun–Thu, 19:00–22:00) or Once on a date.
  • Ready-made presets: Morning focus, Evening study, Wind down (overnight) and Weekend detox.
  • Each schedule has its own start time, length (30 minutes to 8 hours, may run past midnight) and exit level.
  • Uses exact alarms and is re-armed after a restart; a schedule that should already be running starts straight away.
  • Skipped, with a notification, if another session is running or the block list is empty.
  • Finished scheduled sessions count toward your daily goal.
How to use it: More › Schedules, or Plan your week on Home. Add your own or tap a preset.
Breaks

A break that ends on time.

Each session has one break of 3, 5 or 10 minutes. The countdown freezes, blocked apps open, and focus resumes on its own when the break ends.

  • The pill turns teal and shows Back now, so you can end the break early.
  • A short alert when the break ends; focus and blocking resume by themselves.
  • Once used, the break button disappears for the rest of the session, extra time included.
  • A break doesn’t remove the exit level: ending a blocking session still needs it, and Locked sessions still can’t end early.
How to use it: Tap the cup on the floating pill, use “Break 5 min” in the notification, or take it from the block screen (which then opens the app you wanted).
Session report

See where focus slipped.

After every session you get a report: finished or ended early, minutes focused, how often you tried to open blocked apps, notifications hidden, when you took your break, and a timeline.

  • Points out your weak spot, for example “Most attempts came 20–30 min in. Next time, try taking your break around minute 20.”
  • For a plain timer without blocking, it shows time spent in apps from your block list instead.
  • The last 60 reports are kept per profile.
  • Sessions ended in the first minute are treated as a mis-tap and not reported.
How to use it: Reports appear on Home (Last session), in Progress (Recent sessions) and in the notification after a blocking session.
Study

Catch the thought. Keep your place.

Tap the time on the floating pill, then +. A small box opens over your PDF while the timer keeps running.

  • Card: a question, an answer and a subject (recent subjects appear as chips). Saved straight to your vault with a tick.
  • No answer yet? Finish later puts it in the Inbox.
  • Look up: a question for later. Its Search button opens only after the session, so it can’t pull you away now.
  • Note: anything else, saved with the session and minute it came from.
  • Quick tip: a Fact written as question :: answer becomes a flashcard straight away.
  • Inbox (Study screen): Make card, Search, Done, or Delete.
How to use it: Pick Card, Look up or Note, type (or use the keyboard’s mic) and save.
Study

Flashcards that come back at the right time.

Your cards use spaced repetition, a simplified version of the SM-2 method used by Anki. Cards you know come back less often; cards you miss come back soon.

  • Tap the card to flip it over in 3D. Swipe right for Good, left for Again, or use the buttons.
  • Ratings: Again (10 minutes), Hard, Good, Easy. Gaps grow 1 day → 3 days → about ×2.5 each time.
  • Optional daily review reminder, sent only when cards are due.
  • All cards: search and filter by subject. Add and edit cards by hand too.
How to use it: Study › Review cards. Read the question, say the answer in your head, flip, then rate.
Study

Question bank and break quiz.

Keep multiple-choice questions in a question bank, and let FocusFloat quiz you during breaks.

  • Add questions by hand, or import a CSV (question, A, B, C, D, answer letter, subject, explanation) or a JSON file.
  • When a break starts, the app offers 3 quick questions. Skip is always there, and you can switch it off on the Study screen.
  • Questions come from your bank, and from cards whose answer is a number (the app makes the wrong options by changing the number).
How to use it: Study › Question bank to add questions or import a file. Study › Practice 5 MCQs any time.
Study

Trust your first answer.

In a quiz you can change your answer before Lock in. FocusFloat quietly records your first pick.

  • The report shows how often your first answer was right when you changed it.
  • It helps you learn whether changing answers in exams helps you or hurts you.
How to use it: Just use the quizzes. After 20 changed answers, open the Second-guessing report on the Study screen.
Home & progress

A goal, a streak and your week at a glance.

Home greets you with a card that changes with the time of day: a sunrise in the morning, sky blue in the afternoon, a starry purple evening.

  • Your streak with a flickering flame, today’s goal as a ring, and one tap to start your usual length.
  • A session counts when it runs to the end and is at least 5 minutes long. Ending early never counts toward the goal or the streak.
  • Streak: consecutive days with at least one finished session. Today keeps yesterday’s streak alive until midnight.
  • Up next shows what needs you: finish setup, a schedule, cards due, the Inbox, the last report, plan your week.
  • Colourful tool tiles with live status, a Study card, and This week with a tick for each day you reached your goal.
  • Confetti once a day when you reach the goal. Progress tab: week bars, a heat grid and recent sessions.
How to use it: Set your daily goal in Profile (1–8 sessions, default 3).
Account & sync

Your progress follows you.

Sign up once and your progress, reports and study material are on every phone or tablet you log in on.

  • Synced: name, daily goal, progress (each device’s days are kept apart and added together), session reports, cards, questions, Inbox notes and quiz answers.
  • Newest change wins per card; deletions reach your other devices. Works offline and catches up when you’re back online.
  • Not synced, on purpose: block lists, schedules, ScrollStop settings and which apps you used.
  • Forgot password sends a reset link; a verification email is sent on sign-up.
  • Delete account removes your online data, your login and the profile’s data on that phone.
How to use it: Open FocusFloat, then Continue with Google or sign up with email and password. Profile shows the last sync and has Sync now.
Updates

New versions come to you.

FocusFloat checks its website for a newer version, at most every six hours, and tells you in Up next on Home.

  • One tap downloads the update with Android’s download manager and opens the installer.
  • Only files from FocusFloat’s own GitHub releases are accepted, and each one must match its published SHA-256 fingerprint.
  • Android also checks that the update is signed with the same key as the app you have.
  • Your progress, cards and settings stay as they are.
  • The first time, Android asks you to allow FocusFloat to install apps.
How to use it: Tap Update available in Up next, or open More › Check for updates.
Look & feel

Light, dark, and calm motion.

One consistent look across the app, with the Inter typeface, soft cards and each tool’s own colour.

  • Screens glide in and out, cards rise in one after another, and everything you tap presses in slightly.
  • Bottom bar: Home, Progress, a big Focus button, Study and More (App Blocker, ScrollStop, Schedules, Setup, Profile, Theme).
  • Respects Android’s “remove animations” setting.
How to use it: More › Theme: Light (default), Dark, or Like phone.
